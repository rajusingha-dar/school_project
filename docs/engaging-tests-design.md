# Making tests engaging with AI — design notes

Saved from a design discussion (2026-10-04). The live demo of this design is the **Test page**
in the app (log in as a parent → **Take a test**). See "Implementation status" at the bottom.

## 1. Three layers, kept separate

| Layer | Job | Who does it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Content** | The question, the options, the correct answer, the topic tag | Humans, or AI-drafted and then human-reviewed |
| **Presentation** | Story, diagram, hints, points, animation | Code, using templates, with AI writing the story text |
| **Intelligence** | Picks the next question, explains mistakes, writes the mentor summary | Rules for adaptivity, AI for the language |

The correct answer is always computed or stored, never "decided" by the AI. AI image generators
draw wrong geometry, so the diagrams should be generated by code (SVG) from the numbers in the
question.

## 2. Where AI helps

| Moment | What AI does | Why a kid cares |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Before the test** | Re-skins the same question into a story (cricket, space, Minecraft) and changes the numbers | The test feels personal, and a friend's test has different numbers |
| **During the test** | Offers hints in steps: concept, then formula, then a worked start | The kid gets unstuck instead of giving up |
| **After each answer** | Explains the specific mistake in simple language ("you divided opposite by hypotenuse, which is sine, not tangent") | Feels like a tutor, not a red cross |
| **After the test** | Spots patterns across questions and writes the mentor summary | "You're strong at X, one fix to try next" |
| **For the parent** | Weekly plain-language summary | Matches the dashboard mentor panel |

This fits CLAUDE.md: the mentor summary is generated from structured result data, not open-ended
chat.

## 3. The process, step by step

| # | Step | Output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | A teacher or admin writes **question templates** with variables (angle, length) and a topic tag | Template, e.g. "ladder of length L at angle A" |
| 2 | AI drafts story versions and distractor options; a human reviews them | Approved question variants |
| 3 | Code builds the **visual spec** for each question (type plus numbers) and renders the SVG | The diagram |
| 4 | The test starts; a simple rule picks the next difficulty (right goes up, wrong goes down) | Question sequence |
| 5 | Each answer is checked against the stored correct answer; time and hints used are recorded | Attempt data |
| 6 | Points are computed by fixed rules | Score — fun, but separate from mastery |
| 7 | Topic mastery is updated from correctness only | The learning-curve data |
| 8 | AI turns the structured results into the kid's feedback and the parent's summary | Text |

**Keep points and mastery separate.** If points feed mastery, a kid who is fast but careless looks
stronger than they are, and the diagnostic gets distorted. Points are for motivation. Mastery is
for truth.

## 4. Example test: "Mission Surveyor" (10 trigonometry questions)

The story: the kid helps a surveyor measure things without climbing them.

| # | Level | Story and visual | Question | Correct | Pts | Topic tag |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Easy | Right triangle with angle θ highlighted | Which side is *opposite* θ? | Side a | 10 | Ratio identification |
| 2 | Easy | Triangle 3-4-5 | sin θ with opposite 3, hypotenuse 5 | 3/5 | 10 | Sine |
| 3 | Easy | Same 3-4-5 triangle | tan θ | 3/4 | 10 | Tangent |
| 4 | Medium | 30-60-90 triangle, slider to drag the angle | sin 30° | 1/2 | 15 | Standard values |
| 5 | Medium | Two angles shown side by side | cos 60° + sin 30° | 1 | 15 | Standard values |
| 6 | Medium | Kite on a 10 m string at 30° | Height of the kite | 5 m | 15 | Sine in use |
| 7 | Medium | Ladder at 45°, foot 3 m from the wall | How high does it reach? | 3 m | 15 | Tangent in use |
| 8 | Hard | Surveyor 20 m from a tower, 60° elevation | Tower height | 20√3 m | 20 | Tangent in use |
| 9 | Hard | "Code-breaker" lock | If sin θ = 3/5 (acute), find cos θ | 4/5 | 20 | Identities |
| 10 | Boss | Lighthouse 30 m high, boat at 30° depression | Distance of boat from the base | 30√3 m | 25 | Tangent in use |

Total: 155 points (3 × 10 + 4 × 15 + 2 × 20 + 25).

## 5. What a run could look like

Points rule: full points for a correct answer, minus 5 for each hint used.

| # | Aarav's answer | Result | Hints | Points |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Side a | Correct | 0 | 10 |
| 2 | 3/5 | Correct | 0 | 10 |
| 3 | 4/3 | **Wrong** | 0 | 0 |
| 4 | 1/2 | Correct | 1 | 10 |
| 5 | 1 | Correct | 0 | 15 |
| 6 | 5 m | Correct | 0 | 15 |
| 7 | 4 m | **Wrong** | 0 | 0 |
| 8 | 20√3 m | Correct | 1 | 15 |
| 9 | 4/5 | Correct | 0 | 20 |
| 10 | 30 m | **Wrong** | 0 | 0 |
| | | | **Total** | **95 of 155** |

**What the diagnostic sees (mastery, ignoring points):**

| Topic | Correct | Mastery |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ratio identification | 1 of 1 | 100% |
| Sine (Q2, Q6) | 2 of 2 | 100% |
| Standard values | 2 of 2 | 100% |
| Identities | 1 of 1 | 100% |
| **Tangent** (Q3, Q7, Q8, Q10) | **1 of 4** | **25%** |

**Mentor message (written from this table):** "Aarav is strong on sine, cosine and the standard
angles. All three slips involved tangent, and the Q3 wrong answer 4/3 shows he's flipping opposite
and adjacent. Try 5 tangent questions with the triangle labelled first."

That one insight is what a report card never gives a parent, and it comes from the topic tags, not
from the points.

## 6. What makes it engaging without cheapening it

| Idea | How it works |
| --- | --- |
| Story theme | One mission per test, with a progress map across 10 stops |
| Interactive visuals | Drag the angle and watch the triangle and the ratio change |
| Hint ladder | Concept, then formula, then first step, each costing a few points |
| Instant, kind feedback | "Close! You used cosine. Here's how to spot tangent." |
| Personal skins | Cricket, space or game themes on the same underlying question |
| Boss question | A final multi-step challenge worth the most points |

## 7. Open decisions

1. **Points are currently out of scope in CLAUDE.md** ("coins/gamification"). The story, visuals,
   hints and kind feedback fit the scope; points and badges would need that rule changed. Decide
   whether they belong in the MVP or later.
2. **Trigonometry is a Class 10 topic; the MVP is Class 7.** The design works for Class 7 with
   angles, ratios and simple geometry. Decide whether the first real question set is Class 7 Maths.

## 8. Effect on the Step 4 data model

If this design is adopted, the schema needs (kept future-proof, per the project decision):

- A **visual spec** on each question (diagram type plus numbers).
- **Hints** stored on each question.
- A **story theme**.
- **Hints used** and **time taken** recorded per answer.
- **Points** stored separately from **mastery**.
- A **misconception tag** on wrong options, so patterns can be detected across answers.

**Corrections made while building:**

- The per-question points originally summed to 150, not 155. The boss question (Q10) is now worth
  **25** points, which makes the total 155 (the table in section 4 has been updated).
- Aarav's run (section 5) is reproduced exactly by the demo and is covered by a test:
  95 of 155 points, tangent at 1 of 4 = 25%.

**Demo limits (deliberate):**

- It uses bundled sample data and saves nothing.
- The correct answers ship to the browser. The real product must grade on the server.
- The mentor message is rule-based. In production the same structured data would be handed to an
  AI model to phrase, with the facts staying deterministic.
- Adaptive difficulty is not in the demo (questions are in a fixed order).
